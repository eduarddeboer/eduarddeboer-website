# Writing voice — Eduard de Boer

This document is the editorial single source of truth for new prose on eduarddeboer.com.

It is based on a cross-section of Eduard's own pre-generative-AI writing, especially:
- ReputatieCoaching podcast transcripts from 2012–2016;
- ReputatieCoaching blog posts from 2012–2017;
- Allround Fotografie service and editorial copy as a second-context cross-check.

The purpose is not to imitate old wording. It is to preserve the underlying way Eduard naturally communicates while writing at his current professional level.

## Core voice

New prose should feel:

- personal;
- direct;
- investigative;
- practical;
- technically precise;
- evidence-led;
- lightly opinionated where justified;
- didactic without sounding like a textbook;
- occasionally dry or self-relativising;
- confident without self-promotion.

Avoid prose that feels:

- corporate;
- abstract;
- over-smoothed;
- generic;
- over-structured;
- promotional;
- artificially provocative;
- conspicuously "AI-written".

## Relationship with the reader

Write to one reader.

Prefer `je` and `jouw` in Dutch unless context requires another form. `Wij` and impersonal institutional language should not become the default.

Prefer:
> Heb je gecontroleerd of deze gegevens ook werkelijk op dezelfde partij betrekking hebben?

Over:
> Organisaties dienen te verifiëren of de beschikbare gegevens onderling consistent zijn.

The reader should feel that Eduard is explaining or investigating something with them, not addressing an anonymous market segment.

## Open concretely

Do not default to a generic sector introduction.

Eduard naturally starts from:
- an observation;
- something that happened;
- a question;
- a discrepancy;
- a practical case;
- a document;
- a number;
- a test;
- something that initially looked right but was not.

Prefer:
> Je leverancier stuurt 38 polygonen, een leveranciersverklaring en drie facturen. Op het eerste gezicht ziet het dossier er keurig uit. Maar uit welk van die 38 percelen kwam het hout dat werkelijk in jouw product is verwerkt?

Avoid:
> De EUDR stelt bedrijven in een complex regelgevingslandschap voor aanzienlijke uitdagingen.

## Show the reasoning

Do not hide the path from evidence to conclusion.

A characteristic Eduard pattern is:

1. this is what I saw;
2. this is what I checked;
3. this did not fit;
4. so I looked further;
5. this is what the evidence supports;
6. this is the remaining uncertainty.

This visible reasoning is especially important in due-diligence, audit and traceability articles.

Do not merely state:
> De traceability is onvoldoende.

Show where it fails:
> De factuur names supplier A, the production record points to processor B and the geolocations belong to producer C. None of that is necessarily wrong. But the dossier still has to show how those three records relate to the same material flow.

## Logic before decoration

Transitions should express reasoning, not just make prose flow.

Words such as `maar`, `dus`, `want`, `toch`, `dan` and `daarom` are natural when they represent a real logical relationship.

Prefer:
> Dat document bestaat dus. Maar wat bewijst het voor deze partij?

Over:
> Daarnaast is het van belang om ook de relevantie van documentatie in ogenschouw te nemen.

Avoid repetitive AI transitions such as:
- daarnaast;
- bovendien;
- tevens;
- tot slot;
- samenvattend kunnen we stellen;
- in het huidige complexe landschap.

## First person is allowed

Eduard is not an invisible technical writer.

Use `ik` where it adds provenance, judgement or practical context:
- I saw;
- I tested;
- I checked;
- I received this question;
- I would not accept this without further evidence;
- I initially thought X, until Y showed otherwise.

Do not add first person merely for personality.

Prefer demonstrated authority over claimed authority.

Avoid:
> Als ervaren EUDR-expert weet ik...

Prefer:
> Als twee documenten verschillende volumes noemen, wil ik eerst weten waarom voordat ik daar een risicoconclusie aan verbind.

## Be concrete

Use actual:
- quantities;
- dates;
- species;
- countries;
- document types;
- order numbers;
- roles;
- legal provisions;
- sequence steps;
- examples.

Prefer:
> 47 polygonen, 12 facturen en drie houtsoorten

Over:
> veel geolocatiedata en diverse documenten.

Concrete detail is part of the voice, not merely evidence decoration.

## Questions are functional

Use questions to:
- involve the reader;
- expose a gap;
- force the next reasoning step.

Good:
> Het perceel is bekend. Maar bewijst dat ook dat het hout in deze zending daar vandaan komt?

Avoid rhetorical questions that exist only as copywriting hooks.

## Be willing to judge — after the evidence

Eduard's natural style is not timid. If something does not add up, say so.

However, especially for legal and regulatory writing:
- distinguish the rule from the interpretation;
- distinguish facts from professional judgement;
- distinguish uncertainty from error;
- do not overstate what evidence can prove.

A strong pattern is:
> Artikel X vraagt Y. In dit dossier zie ik Z. Dat betekent niet automatisch dat het dossier non-compliant is, maar deze stap is nog niet aantoonbaar gesloten.

## Explain jargon in passing

Do not build a glossary before the reader can enter the article.

Use the correct technical term, explain it briefly at first meaningful use, and continue.

Example:
> De Due Diligence Statement (DDS) is de verklaring die in het EUDR Information System wordt ingediend. Dat is het eindpunt van een proces, niet het bewijs dat het proces goed is uitgevoerd.

## Sentence and paragraph rhythm

Eduard's historical prose generally uses complete, medium-length sentences rather than ultra-short LinkedIn fragments.

Target rhythm:
- mostly medium-length sentences;
- occasional short sentence for emphasis;
- occasional longer sentence when a chain of reasoning requires it;
- compact online paragraphs.

Do not write:
> Dit klopt niet.
>
> Punt.
>
> Waarom?
>
> Simpel.

unless that cadence genuinely fits the moment.

Historic samples became somewhat more concise over time. Modern copy should follow the later, tighter form rather than reproducing 2012-era sentence length.

## Humour and self-relativisation

Dry humour is allowed in small doses.

It should be incidental, not performed.

Good:
> Een bestand met de naam `EUDR FINAL FINAL v3 DEFINITIEF.xlsx` stelt mij op zichzelf nog niet gerust.

Self-correction and admission of earlier assumptions are also natural when true:
> Op het eerste gezicht dacht ik dat dit netjes aansloot. Toen ik de volumes naast elkaar zette, bleek dat toch anders te liggen.

Do not turn technical articles into comedy.

## Punctuation

Historic writing used more exclamation marks and ellipses than is appropriate for the current professional site.

Modernise rather than erase the voice:
- use exclamation marks sparingly;
- use ellipses only when they add natural spoken cadence;
- do not manufacture enthusiasm with punctuation.

## Anti-AI red flags

Rewrite any paragraph containing avoidable phrases such as:
- in het complexe regelgevingslandschap;
- cruciaal;
- robuust en toekomstbestendig;
- holistische aanpak;
- proactief inzetten op;
- kansen en uitdagingen;
- navigeren door;
- stakeholders;
- in een snel veranderende wereld;
- samenvattend kunnen we stellen;
- het is belangrijk om te benadrukken;
- organisaties doen er goed aan.

These phrases are not absolutely prohibited where they are technically necessary, but they should trigger review.

Also review:
- perfectly symmetrical sections;
- repeated three-item bullet groups;
- paragraphs that merely restate the preceding paragraph;
- conclusions that summarize everything without adding judgement;
- abstract claims unsupported by an example;
- generic calls to action.

## EUDR, EUTR and due-diligence writing

For regulatory articles, default to this order:

1. Start with the practical situation or evidence problem.
2. Identify the legal or procedural requirement.
3. Show what the available evidence actually establishes.
4. Identify the missing link, uncertainty or contradiction.
5. Explain the consequence for risk assessment or traceability.
6. Only then state the practical conclusion.

The desired reader reaction is not:
> This is a complete summary of the EUDR.

It is:
> This person understands how to test whether a due-diligence dossier actually closes.

## Authority positioning

The site should make expertise visible through analysis, not through repeated self-description.

Preferred positioning:
- supply-chain due diligence;
- regulatory compliance;
- traceability;
- evidence;
- audit;
- legality;
- risk assessment.

EUDR can be the current subject through which that broader capability is demonstrated.

## Editing test

Before publication, review every substantive paragraph against these questions:

1. Would Eduard plausibly say this?
2. Is it concrete enough?
3. Is the logic visible?
4. Does the evidence support the strength of the statement?
5. Is there unnecessary corporate or AI-like phrasing?
6. Could one abstract sentence be replaced by a real example?
7. Is `je` more natural here than an impersonal construction?
8. Is the paragraph doing new work, or merely summarising?
9. If an opinion is stated, is the basis clear?
10. Does the text sound like Eduard now, rather than an imitation of Eduard in 2014?

## Calibration rule

This guide is a starting model, not a fossil.

For the first new cornerstone articles, feedback such as:
- "dit zou ik nooit zo zeggen";
- "hier zou ik stelliger zijn";
- "dit is te glad";
- "dit is te jolig";
- "ik zou eerst uitleggen hoe ik dit weet";

should be used to refine this file.

Current target:

> Eduard's natural voice + current technical maturity + tighter modern editing.
